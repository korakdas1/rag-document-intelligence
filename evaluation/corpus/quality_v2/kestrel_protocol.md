# Kestrel field protocol: revision B

Record KFS-PB, 21 May 2024. Field manual for the original seasonal schedule. The September amendment names the later cadence change. Historical measurements retain the method in effect when they were acquired.

## Reference observations

Kestrel uses a manual rain gauge as the reference for its automatic precipitation channel.
The reference gauge is read during a staffed visit and checked for blockage before its value is accepted. A blocked gauge is flagged; staff do not assume that a low value proves little rain. The automatic channel can continue recording between visits, but that does not make every interval independently validated. Reference availability is recorded separately from instrument uptime.

Kestrel's Vale Beck valley reference plot is at an elevation of 260 m.
The valley plot is sheltered by a service embankment and is not an interchangeable replacement for the exposed crest. Its easier access helps maintain the reference equipment during difficult weather. Comparisons should identify both locations rather than refer to one unnamed station baseline. A location-specific observation must not inherit the altitude of another site in the same project.

## Original cadence

Kestrel revision B requires one environmental sample every 15 minutes.
The schedule applies to the environmental logger, not to the manual gauge visits. Event alarms can create additional diagnostic messages between regular samples. Those messages are not extra scheduled observations and must not change the denominator used to describe sample completeness. A later cadence change requires a named method revision in the export.

## Controls and handling

Kestrel pairs each humidity exposure run with a sealed blank to check handling contamination.
The blank travels with the exposure containers but remains closed in the field. Its label identifies the trip and handling route, not a sensor channel. Staff must not treat a clean blank as proof that the environmental instrument is accurate. It answers a narrower question about the handling process, while the reference observations address measurement behavior.

Before loading the transport box, the collector checks the lid gasket and logs any visible damage. The box is opened in the receiving area rather than beside the wet-weather bench. A damaged sleeve remains with its original label so that the custody reviewer can reconstruct what happened. Relabeling a sample to make the package look complete is prohibited even when the likely identity seems obvious.

## Inspection and power records

The inspection form separates a visual check, a reference comparison, and a configuration readback. Completing one field does not imply that the others passed. A clean shield can still surround a drifting sensor, and a correct configuration can still be attached to the wrong channel name. Reviewers should ask which check supports a claim instead of accepting an undifferentiated pass mark.

Power records distinguish instantaneous watts from integrated energy. The logger, heater, and charging panel have different operating cycles. Multiplying a peak panel rating by a full day would overstate delivered energy. The operations notebook records the heater's measured interval, while the amendment corrects a unit in the summary. Neither record supplies an unmeasured battery state after the storm.

## Field limits

The protocol excludes salt exposure and deliberate icing trials. Weather during the season may be wet and cold, but incidental conditions are not a controlled qualification test. The study cannot assign performance to an exposure it did not define and observe. Unlogged maintenance intervals remain missing, and no synthetic reading is inserted merely to keep a regular cadence in a plot.
