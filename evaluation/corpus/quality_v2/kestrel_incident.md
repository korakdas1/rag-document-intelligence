# Kestrel storm access and restoration note

Record KFS-IS, 30 July 2024. Incident review for the fictional Northfen Spur site. This note has no priority over the independently maintained operations notebook for the disputed restoration duration.

## Access interruption

Rain deposited loose gravel across the service track. The crew waited for a route inspection before taking a vehicle to the crest. The station continued logging locally during part of the communications interruption. Loss of a remote display therefore did not establish that every instrument had lost power. The site visit checked the power lead and copied the local buffer before restarting the link.

The Kestrel incident note records 9 hours to restore telemetry after the 26 July outage.
The notebook gives another duration for the same outage, with restoration defined in both records as the first stable remote telemetry stream. Neither record supersedes the other. The team had not signed a common reconstruction when the incident review closed. The disagreement is not explained by one author timing vehicle access and the other timing telemetry.

## Observation limits

Kestrel excludes fog-wetted shield readings from its temperature-bias estimate.
That rule does not mean the excluded readings are known to be wrong by a fixed amount. Wetting changes the measurement condition, and the reference comparison was not designed to estimate that condition separately. The crew preserved the flagged readings so later work could inspect them without treating them as accepted temperature evidence.

The visit log does not state the battery's remaining capacity after the storm. Seeing the logger restart is evidence of operation at that moment, not a quantitative state-of-charge measurement. The review also contains no equipment purchase cost or biological census.
