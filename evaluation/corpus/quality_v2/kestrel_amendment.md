# Kestrel seasonal method amendment C

Record KFS-AC. This signed notice changes the sampling cadence and corrects a heater-energy unit. It preserves the original documents so that historical observations can be interpreted under their own methods.

## Effective method

The Kestrel cadence amendment took effect on 3 September 2024.
The crew records that date in the export manifest and checks the logger configuration at the next staffed visit. The date is not the station's founding date or the date of the July storm. Earlier readings are not resampled to make the whole season appear to have used one interval. A mixed-method analysis should retain the boundary explicitly.

Kestrel's current sampling interval is 12 minutes, superseding revision B's 15-minute interval.
This is the regular environmental sampling interval. Diagnostic events and manual reference visits keep their own schedules. The change does not create new observations in historical gaps. Any analysis that compares completeness across the boundary must use the applicable expected sample count rather than one denominator for the whole season.

The shorter Kestrel interval was adopted to capture brief rain transitions between scheduled observations.
Field diaries described showers that began and ended between regular snapshots. More frequent sampling reduces that temporal blind spot while increasing buffer use, so crews also check available storage at each visit. The amendment does not claim that taking more readings automatically improves the accuracy of each reading or captures every transient event.

## Calibration record

The Kestrel amendment's calibration record key is KFS-C7.
The key identifies the readback sheet accompanying the method change. It is not a sample serial or a code for the entire station. If the key is absent, a technician should retrieve the readback sheet rather than infer a configuration from the date on a photograph. The signed sheet preserves both the requested interval and the interval read from the logger.

## Energy correction

Kestrel's measured dew-heater energy was 0.64 kWh per day; the notebook's 0.64 MWh per day entry is a unit error and is superseded.
The numeric part was transcribed correctly, but the unit prefix was not. The source meter export and interval length support the corrected unit. This correction concerns heater energy over the observed daily interval. It is not a peak-power rating for the panel or a measurement of remaining battery capacity after an outage.

Staff attach the correction to copies of the notebook summary without deleting the original text. Readers comparing versions can therefore explain why two incompatible-looking units appear in the archive. A current-value answer should use the corrected unit and identify the earlier entry as erroneous if it is discussed.

## Unresolved and excluded matters

This amendment does not reconcile the competing July telemetry-restoration durations. Neither incident record gains authority from being stored beside this notice. A separate joint reconstruction is still required. The change also makes no claim about sensor behavior under deliberate icing, biological populations near the station, or unrecorded equipment costs.
