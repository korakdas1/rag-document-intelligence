# Atlas Retrieval Research Program: study charter

Record ARP-B, 2 September 2024. Original synthetic research charter. Atlas is a fictional document-retrieval study, and its figures are not measurements of the software hosting this archive.

The Atlas study identifier is ARP-R9.
The study examines how a manual archive handles revisions, identifiers, and competing descriptions of the same equipment. Its working corpus consists of authored engineering manuals. The goal is to make retrieval decisions traceable to a document version, rather than to produce one accuracy number detached from its source collection.

Davin Sol chairs the Atlas research review.
The chair approves the study protocol after the corpus custodian confirms the frozen inventory. Reviewers who label relevance do not administer the retrieval runs. That separation helps preserve a reference judgment when a system result is surprising. A disagreement about a label is recorded as a disagreement, not resolved by adopting the system's top-ranked passage.

The archive contains current manuals, retired manuals, and release notes. Retired material can be useful for historical questions even when it is wrong for a current-value question. Document status therefore travels with every exported passage. A familiar equipment name is not enough to identify the intended version.

The research package does not specify a deployed language model or a commercial billing arrangement. It records a bounded retrieval exercise and its audit process. Hardware power, model parameter counts, and downstream clinical use are outside the recorded experiment. Those omissions must not be filled from assumptions about other systems called Atlas.
