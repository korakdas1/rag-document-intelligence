# Kestrel Field Station: seasonal research brief

Record KFS-B, issued 8 May 2024. Original synthetic field-science material about a fictional station and landscape. The names and measurements are authored for this archive.

## Research purpose

Sora Finn is the lead scientist for the Kestrel wet-weather monitoring study.
The study compares shielded environmental sensors with a maintained reference site. Its purpose is to understand exposure and observation limits during a wet season. It is not a wildlife survey, a weather forecast service, or a certification of equipment for every climate. The field team collects enough operational context to explain a gap without replacing that gap with a guessed observation.

The station operates a crest site and a valley reference plot. Both use terms such as baseline, calibration, and sample, but a sample can mean a logged reading or a sealed physical control depending on the section. Field labels therefore include the material or channel type. A copied number without its location and unit is not a complete measurement.

## Sites and responsibilities

Kestrel's Northfen Spur crest site is at an elevation of 620 m.
The crest is reached by a service track that can become inaccessible after rain. A delayed visit is recorded as delayed maintenance rather than evidence that the sensor failed. The valley reference remains accessible by another route, but its observations cannot fill a missing crest reading simply because the sites share the same project name.

Ivo Marr reviews Kestrel's field logs and reports unresolved exceptions to Sora Finn.
The technician who collected a sample cannot close their own custody exception. Ivo checks the field label, transport sleeve, and receipt entry before escalating an unresolved mismatch. Sora decides whether the affected sample is usable for the study. This reporting relationship is distinct from the volunteer coordinator's responsibility for vehicle access.

## Scope of the season

The team measures environmental conditions and equipment behavior at specified locations. It does not attach the observations to a species inventory or estimate animal abundance from sensor uptime. Equipment energy records describe the heater and logger under stated conditions. They are not a purchasing quotation, and a peak panel rating is not the same thing as daily energy delivered to a load.

The method uses a fixed collection cadence until an explicit amendment takes effect. Staff keep the old method available because a mixed-season archive can contain readings acquired under both schedules. A timestamp should therefore be interpreted together with the applicable method revision. The most recent file in a directory is not necessarily the method that governed an earlier sample.

## Review package

The seasonal package includes a protocol, an operations notebook, a storm incident note, and an amendment. The notebook distinguishes measured heater energy from a unit error in its original summary. The storm note preserves a separate unresolved disagreement over restoration time. A correction to one numeric field must not be extended to unrelated records without an explicit statement of authority.

A project summary should identify which observations were measured, which were inferred, and which were not collected. This is especially important when a missing interval coincides with an access problem. An attractive continuous plot can hide that distinction; the signed field ledger cannot.
