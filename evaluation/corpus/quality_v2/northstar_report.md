# Northstar warm-room operations report

Record NBP-OR, laboratory record closed 14 June 2024. This original synthetic report follows a fictional battery pilot from receipt to archive. Measurements describe this run only. Final acceptance and production authorization are recorded separately in the signed amendment.

## 1. Receipt and start of operations

Northstar receiving staff logged 52 cells before screening exclusions.
The trolley contained individually wrapped cells, transport witnesses, and replacement sleeves. Staff counted sealed objects before opening any packaging. The receiving count therefore describes what arrived, not the final number entered into the cycling analysis. The two counts appear on separate sheets to prevent a reviewer from mistaking an inventory change for experimental attrition.

The Northstar warm-room cycling run began on 11 March 2024.
An earlier commissioning session exercised the empty rack without study cells. Its traces remain in the equipment folder but are excluded from the run log. The start date refers to the first accepted loaded cycle, not to delivery, calibration, or later approval. Operators made that distinction in the handover because all four events involved a validation signature.

The Northstar thermal enclosure alarm is set to 32 degrees Celsius.
The alarm is a protective surface-temperature trigger, not the preparation baseline or a claim about the cell core. An alarm suspends the automated sequence and requires inspection before restart. Resetting the display does not itself clear the hold. The observer records the channel name, current state, and physical condition before deciding whether the sequence can safely resume.

## 2. Bench layout and label control

The warm room contains a working rack and a spare fixture. Their front panels share the same display layout, which makes a photograph useful for orientation but inadequate for identifying a trace. Every export carries the rack serial, holder position, and sample serial. A holder moved after randomization receives a movement note. The note accompanies the result even when the movement appears irrelevant to the final retention value.

Cable routing was inspected before the first loaded cycle. Leads were dressed away from the lid hinge and given enough slack for the observer to see the latch without pulling on it. The contractor proposed bundling the leads tightly for a neater enclosure. Staff declined that arrangement because it would hide an individual lead during inspection. Ease of inspection was treated as an operating requirement rather than a cosmetic preference.

Transport witnesses were kept on a separate tray. They were photographed at receipt and after unpacking but were not connected to the cycling rack. A witness records handling conditions; it is not a substitute control cell. If a sample later showed an unusual trace, staff checked the witness record for context without assigning its observations to the sample's electrical channel.

## 3. Calibration and time alignment

Before each shift, the observer compared the rack clock with the acquisition computer. A clock mismatch was written into the log before correction, so the original ordering could still be recovered. The report uses elapsed cycle time for retention plots and wall-clock time for incident reconstruction. Those representations should not be interchanged when estimating the duration of an interruption.

The calibration indicator was exercised with its own fixture. The resulting current step is larger than the quiet-load signal and appears clearly in the raw trace. Operators marked the interval explicitly instead of relying on a downstream filter to guess its boundaries. Removing a calibration interval from an average is valid only when the removed interval remains available for inspection in the raw record.

The reference meter was connected after the enclosure reached a stable state. Staff avoided moving the lid while taking a quiet-load reading, since the display backlight and alarm indicator could change the measured load. A second observer recorded the equipment state in plain language. The state note prevents an apparently precise current value from being detached from the condition under which it was measured.

## 4. Cycling observations

The charge, rest, and discharge intervals were logged independently. A cycle was not accepted merely because the software advanced its counter. The observer checked that the rest interval completed and that the discharge endpoint was recorded. Incomplete cycles remain visible in the operational record but are excluded from the retained-capacity summary. Their absence from the summary is not evidence that no interruption occurred.

Some holders showed a gradual shift in the surface probe after repeated lid openings. Staff checked the tape placement against the setup photograph before replacing a probe. A replacement created a new channel annotation rather than overwriting the old channel name. This distinction matters when comparing a physical change in the sample with a change in how the sample was observed.

The retained-capacity analysis used the accepted loaded cycles and the corresponding initial capacity for each cell. Staff did not normalize every cell by the largest initial value in the rack. That would favor one position and obscure cell-level variation. The worksheet therefore preserves a row for each serial, a flag for excluded cycles, and a separate summary row for the screened set.

## 5. Results and operating states

Northstar's measured retained capacity was 81.4% after 240 accepted cycles.
This is an observed result for the warm-room screened set. It must not be substituted for a required threshold or presented as a guarantee after years of service. The cycling sequence compresses an operational question into laboratory conditions. Calendar aging, repeated transport, and undocumented downstream loads could change field behavior in ways this run does not measure.

The Northstar pack's quiet standby current was 3.6 mA with the indicator off.
The calibration fixture and its indicator exercise are a different operating state. The two traces use the same current unit and can appear in the same export, but they are not competing estimates of a single value. A report that drops the operating-state qualifier can be numerically faithful while still answering the wrong engineering question.

Observers reviewed the result table against the serial ledger before signing the summary. They checked that a missing row represented an exclusion rather than a sorting error. The review did not involve changing a measured value to match an acceptance threshold. The approval sheet consumes the result; it does not define the result retrospectively. This separation allows later acceptance revisions without inventing a new experimental history.

## 6. Inspection practice and open discrepancy

For the Northstar M2 warm-room bench, the operations schedule requires a harness inspection every 160 cycles.
The protocol lists another interval for the same bench and operating condition. Neither record establishes priority for this schedule, and no reconciliation was signed before this report closed. The operators logged the inspections they actually performed. Those event records should not be used to silently rewrite either stated requirement.

At an inspection, staff looked for abrasion near the latch, checked the strain relief, and compared the cable route with the setup photograph. A passed visual check was recorded as a visual check only. It could not prove continuity under movement. The dropout investigation later demonstrated why a stationary observation and a moving-lead test need separate labels even when they concern the same component.

The open schedule discrepancy was carried to the review register. Its presence does not invalidate every measurement, but it prevents an unqualified statement that one documented inspection rule governed the entire run. A reviewer can report both intervals and the unresolved status without guessing which author intended to supersede the other. An explicit signed correction would be needed to settle that question.

## 7. Dropout handling

When the monitoring channel became flat, the observer stopped the affected holder and preserved the trace. The other holders were checked independently before work resumed. Staff did not fill the missing interval with the mean of neighboring channels. Shared room conditions can explain similar trends, but they cannot establish what an unobserved cell did during a disconnected interval.

The preliminary memorandum recorded the initial component hypothesis. This report records the operational response rather than a final root cause. The later amendment is the location for the closed finding. Retaining all three documents lets a reviewer distinguish observation, hypothesis, and correction. Searching for the word failure without the document status would conflate those different kinds of statement.

## 8. Interpretation boundary

The pilot provides evidence about an attended warm-room setup. It contains no measured cold-room capacity series. Staff also did not translate the cycling result into a warranty period or a project budget. Commercial and long-term service questions require additional records. An absent field in this report should remain absent in a summary, even if a plausible value is familiar from another project.

The report deliberately keeps raw measurements beside their caveats. That can produce several nearby percentages, currents, and dates in a search result. The reader must identify which quantity the question asks for and retain the relevant condition. A statement about the calibration fixture, for example, cannot stand in for a statement about quiet pack operation simply because both mention the same rack.

## 9. Archive and closeout

Northstar raw current traces are retained for 90 days after laboratory closeout.
The retention interval concerns the acquisition traces, not the signed decision record. The custodian produces a manifest before any routine expiration and checks that the final incident finding points to the correct trace bundle. Exporting a summary table does not extend or shorten the raw-trace interval. If a hold is issued, it is recorded as a separate archive instruction.

Northstar closeout requires a photograph of the sealed sample envelope.
The photograph links the physical handover to the serial ledger. It is not a substitute for the electrical result or a claim that the sample is safe for production. The receiving custodian checks the visible seal against the manifest before acknowledging transfer. If the image is unreadable, the envelope remains in local custody until the record can be completed.

The final handover includes the acquisition manifest, the exclusion ledger, and links to the protocol and amendment. Staff check the links by opening them from the exported package, not merely by reading the filenames. This avoids a complete-looking package whose links still point to a technician's local directory. Archive completeness and experimental acceptance remain separate checks with separate signatures.
